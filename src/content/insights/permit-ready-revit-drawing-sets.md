Nothing slows a project like a permit set that comes back with a page of comments. Most resubmissions are not about design. They come from **missing information or drawings that contradict each other.** A well-organised Revit set prevents both, because every plan, section and schedule comes from the same coordinated model.

Requirements vary by city and county, so always check your local jurisdiction's checklist. The structure below covers what most residential and small commercial permits ask for.

## The typical sheet list

### General

- **Cover sheet:** project data, scope of work, code analysis, sheet index, consultants and contact details
- **General notes and abbreviations**

### Architectural

- **Site plan:** property lines, setbacks, easements, existing and proposed structures, utilities and lot coverage
- **Floor plans:** dimensions, room names, door and window tags, wall types
- **Roof plan:** slopes, drainage and roof materials
- **Exterior elevations:** heights, materials and finished grades
- **Building sections:** floor-to-floor heights, assemblies and insulation
- **Details:** key wall, roof, stair and waterproofing details
- **Schedules:** doors, windows and finishes

### Other disciplines (as required)

- Structural drawings and calculations
- Energy compliance documentation (for example Title 24 in California)
- Mechanical, electrical and plumbing plans

## How to organise the set in Revit

1. **Start from a template** with your title block, sheet numbering, text styles and view templates already set.
2. **Use view templates** so every plan, elevation and section at the same scale looks the same.
3. **Let schedules come from the model.** Door and window schedules that read from model elements cannot disagree with the plans.
4. **Tag from the model, not with text.** Tags update when the design changes; typed text does not.
5. **Control revisions** with Revit's revision tools, so every reissued sheet shows what changed and when.

## A pre-submission checklist

- Every sheet listed in the index exists, and every existing sheet is in the index
- Dimensions on plans match sections and elevations
- Door and window tags match the schedules
- Heights and setbacks shown on the site plan match the zoning analysis
- Notes reference the current code editions used by the jurisdiction
- No old revision clouds or "draft" stamps left on issued sheets
- PDFs are exported at the correct sheet size and scale
